Getting to Japanese Ski Resorts by Train

Written by Schi, working ski instructor (SAJ Crown Prize)
Updated 2026-11-16
Ad: this page contains affiliate links.

Why train

Winter roads in Nagano, Niigata and Hokkaido are icy and most rental cars come with studless tires that foreign drivers have never used. The shinkansen (bullet train) runs on time in snow, and the resorts built around it exist because Japanese skiers have been doing day trips from Tokyo for forty years. This article lists the resorts in YukiNavi that you can reach by shinkansen and a short bus, shuttle or walk, and names the ones where you will need a taxi.

How the data was selected

I took resorts marked reachable by public transport in the app whose access text names a shinkansen station, then added the resorts whose nearest station is Echigo-Yuzawa or Jomo-Kogen, both Joetsu Shinkansen stops. Prices are the adult one-day lift pass in the data. Shuttle times are from the resorts' own access pages as recorded in the app.

Tokyo side: Echigo-Yuzawa

Echigo-Yuzawa is the easiest ski station in Japan. In winter the Joetsu Shinkansen continues one stop to Gala-Yuzawa, a station that opens directly into the resort. Most other Yuzawa resorts run free shuttles from the East or West exit.

Hakuba is reached from Nagano station by express bus, about 1 hour to Hakuba47 Winter Sports Park and 1 hour 15 minutes to Hakuba Goryu Snow Resort. It works as a train trip but the bus leg is not short. Shiga Kogen Mountain Resort is a direct Nagaden bus from Nagano station East Exit.

Tokyo side: Jomo-Kogen and Minakami

Jomo-Kogen is the Joetsu Shinkansen stop before the Yuzawa tunnel. The Gunma resorts behind it are quieter and cheaper than Yuzawa, but the last legs are longer and several shuttles need a reservation.

Zao Onsen itself is a bus from Yamagata station, about 40 minutes. In Hokkaido the shinkansen ends at Hakodate, so Sapporo-area resorts are reached by plane plus bus. Hoshino Resorts Tomamu Ski Resort has its own JR station served by limited express trains from Sapporo, with a free 5 to 10 minute shuttle.

Where you will need a taxi

Many smaller resorts sit 5 to 40 minutes from a local station with no bus. YukiNavi marks 16 resorts as taxi-only from the station and another 10 as having no public transport option listed. Examples with the taxi time from the app:

  • Chateraise Ski Valley Nobeyama: 5 min from Nobeyama Sta., about ¥1,500-1,600.
  • Kurohime Kogen Snow Park: 10 min from Kurohime Sta.
  • Niseko Moiwa Ski Resort: 10 min from Niseko Sta.
  • Yomase Onsen Ski Area: 12 min from Yudanaka Sta.
  • Mt.KOSHA X-JAM Takaifuji: 20 min from Yudanaka Sta.
  • Yunomaru Ski Area: 25 min from Shigeno Sta.; the bus route was discontinued.
  • Minenohara Kogen Ski Resort: 35 min from Ueda Sta.
  • Family Gelande Kirigamine Ski Area: 40 min from Kami-Suwa Sta.

Rural taxis are not waiting at every station. Ask your accommodation to book one, or call the number posted at the station. Agree on a pick-up time for the return, because the last local train leaves early and you will have skis.

Practical points

  • Reserve shinkansen seats for weekend mornings in January and February; the Tokyo to Echigo-Yuzawa trains fill with skiers.
  • Oversize baggage rules apply on the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu shinkansen. Skis in a bag are allowed on the Joetsu and Hokuriku lines, but put them in the space behind the last row.
  • Where the app says reservation required for a shuttle, it means the day before at the latest. Several Gunma and Fukushima shuttles do not run at all without bookings.
  • Check the return shuttle time before you buy the lift pass. At a two-round-trip resort, missing the afternoon bus means a long taxi.
