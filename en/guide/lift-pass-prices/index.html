Lift Pass Prices in Japan 2026-27: What to Expect

Written by Schi, working ski instructor (SAJ Crown Prize)
Updated 2026-11-16
Ad: this page contains affiliate links.

The short version

A lift pass is リフト券 (rifuto-ken). Across the 355 resorts in YukiNavi that list an adult one-day price, the median is ¥4,800. Half of all resorts charge between ¥3,140 and ¥6,000. The resorts foreign visitors have heard of charge two to three and a half times the median. Of the 403 resorts, 186 have confirmed 2026-27 prices; the rest still carry last season's figure until the resort publishes an update, so treat them as a floor.

Kanto is the most expensive region on median because every resort there is a Tokyo day trip and priced for it. Tohoku is the best value for the terrain you get: Zao Onsen at ¥8,000 and Appi Kogen at ¥9,500 are the top of the region, and the prefectural median in Yamagata is ¥3,450.

The cheapest and the most expensive

The three cheapest passes in the data are town-run hills in northern Hokkaido: Rishiri Tenbozan Ski Area ¥300, Nakagawa Town Ski Area ¥400, Sarufutsu Village Ski Area ¥500. They have one or two lifts and exist for local schoolchildren. They are not a destination, but they show what a lift ride costs when nobody is paying for a resort village.

The top of the list is all Hokkaido and Nagano: Rusutsu Resort ¥16,700, Niseko United (All Mountain Pass) ¥13,500, Niseko Moiwa Ski Resort ¥13,000, Niseko Tokyu Grand Hirafu and Niseko Hanazono Resort ¥12,500, Niseko Village Ski Resort ¥10,700, then Karuizawa Prince Hotel Ski Resort, Hakuba Goryu Snow Resort and Hakuba47 Winter Sports Park at ¥10,000.

Niseko and Rusutsu use seasonal pricing. The app records the peak-season window price. The deals data shows the Niseko United pass at ¥8,800 at the window in early season against ¥13,500 from December 24 to February 28, so the date you travel changes the price by more than any discount.

My view as an instructor: for a first or second week on skis, the ¥5,000 to ¥7,000 band buys more useful skiing per yen than Niseko does. You pay the Niseko premium for off-piste access and an English-speaking town, not for the beginner slope.

Buying online

154 of the 403 resorts have an online ticket page recorded in YukiNavi. The discount for buying in advance is usually small but consistent:

  • Niseko United All Mountain Pass: ¥12,800 online against ¥13,500 at the window in peak season, about 5 percent. A ¥500 key card fee applies on the first purchase.
  • GALA Yuzawa Snow Resort: ¥7,500 online, ¥8,000 at the window.
  • Karuizawa Prince Hotel Ski Resort: ¥9,500 online, ¥10,000 at the window in the main season; ¥7,500 to December 18.
  • Sapporo Kokusai Ski Resort: ¥6,700 online, ¥7,000 at the window.
  • Furano Ski Resort: ¥500 off at the web price.
  • Kiroro Snow World: 5 percent off on the official web store.

Early-bird tickets sold before the season are the bigger saving. In the deals data: Hakuba Iwatake Snowfield ¥5,700 (normally ¥7,500), Manza Onsen Ski Resort ¥4,500 (normally ¥6,300), Hunter Mountain Shiobara ¥4,200 (normally ¥6,500). Most close at the end of November. The Hakuba Happo-one early-bird at ¥6,400 instead of ¥9,800 requires a Japanese My Number Card, so it is for residents only.

Many online tickets are issued through QR codes at a self-service machine at the resort, and some vendors (Webket, Asoview, WAmazing) have English pages. Buy by the day before where the app says so; dynamic-priced resorts like Ishiuchi Maruyama Snow Resort change the web price daily.

Student and youth prices

85 resorts list a separate student price. The median saving against the adult pass is ¥1,200, about 22 percent. Examples: Shiga Kogen Mountain Resort ¥6,500 against ¥9,500, Niseko United ¥11,500 against ¥13,500, Rusutsu Resort ¥12,500 against ¥16,700, Ishiuchi Maruyama Snow Resort ¥4,200 against ¥8,500 on its online student plan. A student card from any country is normally accepted, but Japanese staff may not recognise it; carry a passport as well.

Yukimaji: free lifts at 19 to 22

Yukimaji (雪マジ) is a Recruit programme that gives people aged 19 to 22 (born April 2, 2004 to April 1, 2008) free one-day passes at 130 participating resorts, up to two times per resort, with discounts after that. It runs from November 5 until each resort closes. You register with a Recruit ID on Jalan and the Maji-bu site, verify by SMS, and show the coupon with original photo ID at the window. The SMS step needs a phone that can receive Japanese verification messages, so in practice it is built for residents and exchange students. If you are studying in Japan at the right age, it is the single biggest saving in the data.

Prince Snow Resorts runs a separate scheme: anyone born April 2, 2006 to April 1, 2007 rides free on weekdays at eight Prince resorts including Furano Ski Resort, Naeba Ski Resort, Kagura Ski Resort, Karuizawa Prince Hotel Ski Resort, Shiga Kogen Mountain Resort (Yakebitai) and Manza Onsen Ski Resort, from November 30 to April 30, excluding weekends, holidays and the New Year period. It uses a coupon in the Seibu Prince Global Rewards app plus photo ID.

What to budget

For a regional resort with a train connection, plan on ¥5,000 to ¥7,000 for the pass. For Hakuba, Shiga or Myoko, ¥9,000 to ¥10,000. For Niseko in peak season, ¥12,500 to ¥13,500 for a one-mountain or all-mountain pass. Rusutsu is in its own band. Buy online where offered, travel outside December 24 to February 28 if you can, and if you are a student bring the card.
