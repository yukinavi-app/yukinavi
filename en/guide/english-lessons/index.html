What the English rating in YukiNavi means

Every resort in YukiNavi carries an English support level. It is a judgement about the resort as a whole, not only the ski school.

  • High: English-speaking staff and English menus or signage are normal. 14 of 403 resorts.
  • Medium: some signage and information in English, staff English varies by desk. 84 resorts.
  • Low: limited English support. 305 resorts.

Separately, the school field records whether the ski school offers lessons in English. 51 resorts have this flag. The two do not always line up. Inawashiro Ski Resort, Kuju Forest Park Snow Resort and Kamui Ski Links are rated low overall but run an English or international school. Rusutsu Resort and Niseko United are rated high but their school entries in the data are not yet filled in, so they are missing from the table below even though English lessons are easy to find there.

An honest note before the list

I teach at a Japanese resort. Outside Niseko, Hakuba and a handful of destination resorts, the typical ski school has one or two instructors who can run a lesson in English, and they are booked first. Ticket windows, rental shops and restaurants at low-rated resorts work in Japanese. You can manage with a translation app and patience, but do not expect it to be like a Canadian or Austrian resort. If you want everything in English, choose from the high group and accept the price.

How to book

Book the lesson before you book the lift pass. English slots at medium-rated resorts sell out on weekends and over the New Year holiday. Use the school link in the app, state the language you need in the first line of the request, and give your height, weight and shoe size if you also need rental. If you are a complete beginner, a half-day group lesson is enough for day one.
